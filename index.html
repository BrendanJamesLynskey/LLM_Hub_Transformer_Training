Glossary

Every training term in the series, in one sentence, with the deck slide that explains it and the site chapter where it is animated.

TermIn one sentenceDeckSite
Training stepOne turn of the loop: one batch, one forward and backward pass, one optimiser update.deck 01ch 1
Parameters (weights)The model's adjustable numbers; training changes nothing else. Part 1's model has 5,376.deck 01ch 1
Next-token predictionEach position predicts the following token from the true text so far (teacher forcing), so a window of S+1 tokens gives S predictions.deck 01ch 3
Cross-entropy lossMean of minus the log probability given to the true next token; its gradient at the logits is p − y.deck 01ch 3
Loss curve and plateauLoss against training step; this model sits at the letter-frequency loss for hundreds of steps before attention pays.deck 01ch 1
Validation lossLoss on held-out text the model never trains on; the honest measure, and what runs are compared on.deck 01ch 1
PerplexityeL: a model with loss L is as unsure as a uniform choice among eL tokens.deck 01ch 3
Seeds and spreadSeeded randomness makes runs reproducible; comparisons use several seeds, mean ± standard deviation.deck 01ch 1
Context length, windows and batchesHow many tokens the model sees at once; a batch is many windows drawn at random from the training text.deck 02ch 2
Byte-pair encoding (BPE)Learn a vocabulary by repeatedly merging the most frequent adjacent pair of symbols.deck 02ch 2
Packing, deduplication, mixturesHow pretraining corpora are prepared: documents packed into windows, near-duplicates removed, sources filtered and mixed.deck 02ch 2
GradientOne number per weight: how fast the loss changes if that weight alone is nudged; recomputed every batch.deck 03ch 4
BackpropagationThe chain rule applied in reverse, operation by operation, from the loss to the first layer.deck 03ch 4
Activation memoryWhat the forward pass must keep for the backward pass; for large models often more than the weights.deck 03ch 10
SGDStep by the learning rate times the gradient.deck 04ch 5
MomentumStep along a running sum of past gradients; the buffer is per-weight optimiser state.deck 04ch 5
Adam (moments, bias correction)Per-weight running means of the gradient (m) and its square (v), corrected for their zero start; step η·m/√v.deck 04ch 5
AdamW (decoupled weight decay)Shrink every weight by (1 − ηλ) directly instead of adding the decay to the gradient.deck 04ch 5
Learning-rate sweepTrain at a ladder of learning rates about 3× apart, several seeds each, and keep the best; done per method.deck 05ch 6
Warmup, cosine and WSDLearning-rate schedules: rise linearly at the start; decay smoothly (cosine) or hold then decay at the end (warmup–stable–decay).deck 05ch 6
Critical batch size, gradient noise scaleBelow it, doubling the batch halves the steps; above it, it barely helps. Estimated by tr(Σ)/|G|2.deck 05ch 6
Pre-LN vs post-LNNorm before each sublayer (stream grows, gradients even) or after each residual sum (stream at 1, needs warmup).deck 06ch 7
µP (maximal update parameterisation)Width-scaled initialisation and learning rates so a narrow model's best learning rate transfers to a wide one.deck 06ch 7
QK-norm and z-lossNormalise queries and keys so attention scores cannot grow with the weights; z-loss keeps the output softmax's log Z near 0.deck 07ch 8
Gradient clippingRescale any gradient whose global norm exceeds a threshold; or skip the batch.deck 07ch 8
BF16 and mixed precision16-bit compute with FP32's exponent range; FP32 master weights and accumulation; loss scaling for FP16.deck 08ch 9
Quantisation, full scale and clippingRound values to a few integer levels between ± a full scale; values beyond it are clipped.deck 08ch 9
Sixteen bytes per parameterMixed-precision Adam: 2 weight + 2 gradient + 8 Adam moments + 4 FP32 master.deck 09ch 10
Activation checkpointingKeep only some activations and recompute the rest in the backward pass: memory for compute.deck 09ch 10
Data parallelismEvery worker holds the whole model and its own share of the batch; gradients are averaged each step.deck 10ch 11
All-reduce, ring, reduce-scatterEvery worker ends with the sum; the ring does it as a reduce-scatter then an all-gather, sending 2(p−1)/p of the vector.deck 10ch 11
All-gatherEvery worker ends with everyone's pieces; per-worker bytes grow with the number of workers.deck 10ch 11
Payload vs wire bytesWhat a worker originates vs what it actually sends in the collective, forwarding included.deck 10ch 11
ZeRO and FSDPShard optimiser states, then gradients, then weights across data-parallel workers.deck 10ch 11
Step time and overlapStep time = compute + communication; overlapping the gradient sync with the backward pass hides it.deck 10ch 11
MFUModel FLOPs utilisation: the model's needed FLOPs per second over the hardware's peak.deck 10ch 11
Pipeline parallelism and the bubbleStages of layers on different devices; idle fill-and-drain time is (p−1)/(vm) of the work.deck 11ch 12
Tensor parallelismSplit each layer's matrices across devices: column then row split, all-reduces every layer.deck 11ch 12
WAN / inter-data-centre bandwidthLinks between sites, far slower than NVLink or a cluster network, and shared.deck 12ch 13
Top-k sparsificationSend only the k largest values (and their positions).deck 12ch 13
Error feedback (residual)Keep what a compressor dropped and add it to the next step's vector, so it is sent later.deck 12ch 13
Compressed all-gatherCompressed vectors differ in layout per worker, so they are all-gathered: wire = (W−1)×payload.deck 12ch 13
DeMo (decoupled momentum)Local momentum per worker; send the top DCT coefficients of each chunk, keep the rest; sign step.deck 12ch 13
DiLoCo / local SGDH local AdamW steps per worker, then an outer Nesterov step on the averaged pseudo-gradient.deck 12ch 13
Queueing and stragglersWaiting for a busy shared link (M/D/1) and for the slowest worker each sync.deck 12ch 13
Scaling laws and IsoFLOP profilesAt fixed compute C ≈ 6ND, loss against model size has a minimum; how it moves with C is the law.deck 13ch 14
ExtrapolationPredicting a run too large to try from fits on small ones; only as good as the assumption that nothing changes.deck 13ch 14
Steps (and time) to a target lossCompare methods by when they first reach a loss fixed in advance, not by final loss.deck 13ch 15
CheckpointWeights, optimiser state, data position and schedule step: enough to resume bit for bit.deck 13ch 15
Goodput and Young's intervalShare of time doing useful work under failures; best checkpoint interval near √(2δM).deck 13ch 15
SFT, RLHF and DPOFine-tuning after pretraining: response-only loss, reward-model RL, or direct preference optimisation.deck 13ch 16
